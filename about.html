My story

Aadya Agarwal is a Princeton, New Jersey student with roots in India. Her name comes from Sanskrit — it means “first” and “excellent,” and echoes the goddess Durga, a symbol of primordial energy and power. She grew up in a lively, multicultural home where unstructured conversation was the norm, and that upbringing shaped how she works: curious across disciplines, quick to include people, and grateful for the relationships that carry an idea forward.

Her favorite book is Charlie Mackesy’s The Boy, the Mole, the Fox and the Horse, which keeps her anchored to a simple goal — to live with gratitude and leave the world a little better than she found it. That outlook runs through everything from her water-remediation research to the poems she publishes.

Away from the lab and the page, Aadya is an enthusiastic adventurer and cook. She has gone parasailing, horseback riding and skiing, spent time on farms, and once — at age ten — made a 20-foot cliff jump into the crystal-clear water of a Mexican cenote. In the kitchen she likes to experiment, including an avocado buttercream she is quietly proud of.

For other young women in STEM

What you can take from this work

Aadya hopes her path is useful to other students — especially young women — who are deciding whether their questions are worth chasing. A few things she has learned along the way:

  • Start before you feel ready. Aadya’s projects began as questions she did not yet know how to answer — a water sample, a sensor, a first line of code — and grew through iteration, not certainty.
  • Let disciplines cross. Her strongest work lives where engineering meets environmental science, writing and community service; the most interesting problems rarely stay inside one subject.
  • Build for the people usually left out. Whether it is a one-minute vitals check at a farmers market or a low-cost water filter, the goal is to make care and opportunity more within reach.
